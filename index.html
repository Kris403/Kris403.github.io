Why should nurses teach safe sleep during the birth hospitalization?

Answer

The hospital stay is the ideal time to:
  • Model safe sleep behaviors
  • Teach SIDS/SUID prevention
  • Help families create a safe sleep environment before discharge
What are the key safety points for skin-to-skin care?

Answer

  • Place baby upright on the parent's chest
  • Keep the infant's face visible at all times
  • Keep the nose and mouth uncovered
  • Avoid slouching or sleeping while holding baby
  • Transfer baby to the bassinet if the parent becomes sleepy
What should parents know about room sharing?

Answer

  • Room sharing is recommended for at least the first 6 months
  • Baby should sleep in their own bassinet, crib, or play yard
  • Use a firm, flat sleep surface
  • Avoid bed-sharing, couches, and recliners for infant sleep
  • Room sharing supports feeding while maintaining a safe sleep environment
Why is rooming-in important?

Answer

Rooming-in helps mothers:
  • Recognize feeding cues
  • Respond quickly to infant needs
  • Practice responsive feeding
  • Improve breastfeeding success
PACER Acronym

PACER

P = Pacifier at naps and bedtime can reduce SIDS risk

A = Avoid early pacifier introduction before breastfeeding is established

C = Check breastfeeding success signs

  • Audible swallowing
  • Weight gain
  • Adequate wet and dirty diapers
  • 8-12 feedings per day

E = Established breastfeeding typically occurs around 3-4 weeks postpartum

R = Reduce SIDS risk with:

  • Breastfeeding
  • Safe sleep practices
  • Pacifier use after breastfeeding is established
30-Second Nurse Teaching Script
"Breastfeeding lowers the risk of SIDS, and pacifier use during sleep may also reduce SIDS risk. To protect milk supply, we recommend waiting until breastfeeding is well established, usually around 3-4 weeks. Signs breastfeeding is established include good weight gain, swallowing during feeds, frequent wet diapers, and feeding 8-12 times each day."
What education should parents receive before formula feeding?
Safe preparation, feeding, handling, storage, and feeding techniques for infant formula.
Why is hand hygiene important when preparing infant formula?
Proper handwashing helps prevent contamination and protects infants from infection.
What infant feeding items should be cleaned after every use?
Bottles, nipples, rings, caps, pumps, brushes, and food preparation surfaces should be cleaned according to manufacturer recommendations.
How should concentrated infant formula be prepared?
Follow the manufacturer's instructions exactly and use the correct amount of water and formula.
Why is accurate measurement important when mixing formula?
Incorrect dilution can lead to poor nutrition, dehydration, or medical complications for the infant.
What are appropriate bottle feeding techniques?
Use paced bottle feeding, maintain eye contact, hold the baby closely, and observe feeding cues.
Why is powdered infant formula a special concern?
Powdered infant formula is not sterile and may contain pathogens that can cause serious illness, especially in infants younger than 2 to 3 months.
How can mothers reduce engorgement if exclusively formula feeding?
Wear a supportive bra, apply ice packs for swelling or discomfort, avoid breast stimulation, and use approved anti-inflammatory medication as directed.
What infant feeding concerns require referral to a healthcare provider?
Poor feeding, dehydration, lethargy, persistent vomiting, inadequate weight gain, or parental concerns regarding feeding.
What question should the admitting nurse ask regarding infant feeding education?
Ask: "Has anyone talked to you about the benefits of breastfeeding or the risks of formula feeding?"
How can a nurse start a conversation about breastfeeding?
"Has anyone talked to you about the benefits of breastfeeding? Do you have any questions about feeding your baby this way?"
What should nurses tell patients about lactation support?
Dedicated lactation consultants and trained staff are available during hospitalization and after discharge to support breastfeeding goals.
How can breastfeeding benefit maternal recovery after birth?
Breastfeeding stimulates oxytocin release, which helps the uterus contract and may reduce postpartum bleeding.
How may breastfeeding benefit maternal mental health?
Breastfeeding supports bonding and may help reduce the risk of postpartum depression in some mothers.
What is colostrum and why is it important?
Colostrum is the first milk produced after birth. It is rich in antibodies and nutrients that help protect newborns from infection and support immune development.
How does colostrum benefit a newborn's digestive system?
Colostrum coats and protects the intestines and acts as a natural laxative to help clear meconium from the baby's gut.
What maternal warning signs should prompt contact with a healthcare professional after discharge?
Persistent painful latch, breast lumps, breast pain, fever, concerns about milk production, profound sadness, aversion toward the child, or any doubts regarding breastfeeding success.
